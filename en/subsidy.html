STACKING

There are actually two pots —
and they stack

Most people know about the implementation subsidy and miss the training grant. They come from different agencies, are applied for separately, and together they push your out-of-pocket cost far lower than you'd expect.

POT 1

Implementation — buy the system, build it

Budgeted by MOEA agencies to cover AI tool licences, system build, consulting and development. Depending on your sector and project size, this typically covers 30–50% of total project cost.

  • Retail / food service: NT$100K per store, up to NT$3M for chains
  • SME manufacturers: up to NT$5M
  • Data-application projects: up to NT$8M total, subsidy ≤ 50%
POT 2

Training — so your staff actually use it

Workforce development funding from the Ministry of Labor and the Industrial Development Bureau, covering courses, instructors and in-house training. It's the pot everyone forgets — and it decides whether anyone touches the system after go-live.

  • Ministry of Labor: up to 70% for digital-transformation courses, ceiling ~NT$2M
  • Under 50 employees: fully funded track with an assigned consultant
  • Industrial Development Bureau: up to ~NT$1M per year
Why stacking matters

AI projects fail for two reasons: it looked too expensive to start, and nobody used it once it was built. The implementation subsidy solves the first; the training grant solves the second. Apply for only one and you've solved half the problem.

BEFORE_YOU_APPLY

Four things worth knowing first

Better to know where you're blocked now than to be rejected later.

01 · Branch offices usually don't qualify

Most programs require an independent legal entity. Branches and representative offices are commonly excluded, so decide early which group company files.

02 · No double-dipping on the same cost

A line item already funded by another government program can't be claimed again. If you have an active project running, tell us up front.

03 · The project lead can't be fully outsourced

Programs require a real internal owner. We can ghostwrite and run the process, but someone on your side needs to be named and to attend the meetings.

04 · Budgets are finite and close early

Each year's budget is fixed, windows have deadlines, and most programs review on a first-come basis. Preparing late usually means missing the round.

FAQ

What owners ask us most

Do you charge extra to handle the application?＋

The diagnostic and eligibility assessment are free. If you decide to apply, the application support is folded into the project quote — and we'll show you the real out-of-pocket cost both with and without funding before you commit.

What if the application is rejected?＋

We always prepare a scaled-down version that pays for itself without a subsidy — start with the piece that has the clearest return, then reapply in the next round. Your project doesn't stall because a grant didn't land.

When does the money actually arrive?＋

Almost all of these are reimbursement-based: you pay, you keep the invoices, and funds are disbursed mid-project or after closeout. Plan the cash flow accordingly — we flag this during proposal stage.

Can I really apply for both pots at once?＋

Yes — different agencies, different budget lines. The one condition is that the same expense can't be claimed twice: system costs go to the implementation subsidy, course costs to the training grant, with clean separation in the books.

We're a small company. Is this still for us?＋

Yes, and arguably more so. Companies under 50 employees have a dedicated fully-funded training track, and the single-store service subsidy has a low barrier. Small firms have simpler processes, so results usually show within a month or two.

Do I have to write the proposal myself?＋

No. We write it against the scoring criteria. You supply company documents, review the content, and sign. If a presentation or defense is required, we go with you.
